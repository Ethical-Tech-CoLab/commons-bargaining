# Commons Collective / CCF: community-led action pilot

**Funding proposal | 2026-09-19 | v0.1**

**Repository-only draft; not published as a website document; not submitted.** This public GitHub repository is not confidential. Human, legal, and financial approval is pending. Commons Collective Federation (CCF) is a proposed institution, not an implemented or funded organization. No named partner, funder, researcher, or other institution has committed to or endorsed this proposal.

**Illustrative request:** $750,000 in grant funding over 24 months. Applicant or fiscal sponsor, U.S. community and jurisdiction, authorized signatory, actual partners, and prospective funder are **TO CONFIRM**. This is a discussion draft, not an approved application or legal advice.

## 1. Purpose and beneficiaries

Support a bounded coalition of four to six libraries, educator organizations, and open educational resource (OER) stewards in one U.S. community. All recruitment numbers are planning targets, not commitments. The coalition would negotiate accountable AI-service purchasing terms and operate a paid, maintained OER service while preserving free reuse of underlying open materials.

Direct beneficiaries would be participating staff, resource maintainers, and approximately 24 compensated community representatives, including affected nonmembers. Public beneficiaries would receive accessible open resources, reusable procurement tools, and documented lessons. The pilot would not process learner records or deliver automated decisions about learners.

## 2. Problem and theory of change

Commons funding already exists through donations, public support, memberships, and paid services. Wikimedia Enterprise illustrates a paid-service model alongside continuing free access; its principles reject customer editorial influence. These precedents establish possibilities, not proof that CCF will work. [S80] [S83]

The unresolved problem is whether a small coalition can secure durable value-return to resource maintenance and meaningful authority over AI-service conditions without charging for freedoms people already possess. Fragmented purchasing, unpaid maintenance, and weak exit options are hypotheses to test locally, not universal empirical claims.

**Theory of change:** compensated community decision-making establishes legitimate priorities; narrow mandates enable accountable joint purchasing; maintained services create something buyers can voluntarily pay for; transparent agreements direct a defined share of eligible receipts toward commons stewardship. Independent assurance tests whether benefits exceed coordination costs and whether participants can refuse, contest, and leave. Collective-choice and monitoring principles inform this design, but cannot substitute for local consent. [S03]

The alternative may be ordinary procurement or existing service providers. If those deliver equivalent agency and maintenance benefits more cheaply, the coalition should use them instead.

## 3. Priority activities and deliverables

1. **Community co-design.** Recruit through accessible local channels, fund participation and accessibility, and map existing maintenance work. Publish a community-approved problem statement, baseline, decision charter, and selection criteria. Participation is voluntary; payment compensates time and work, never preferred answers, permission sales, or agreement with CCF.
2. **Scoped mandates.** Develop plain-language, nonexclusive authorizations specifying purpose, material, duration, decision authority, and prospective withdrawal. Purchasing authority and any contributor authorization remain separate. Confirm rights before inclusion; a representative cannot authorize somebody else's rights. Withdrawal cannot retroactively cancel valid CC licenses. [S08]
3. **Collective purchasing.** Run a competitive process for a bounded staff-facing AI resource-discovery or cataloguing service using only approved open materials. Negotiate price, support, portability, supplier-use restrictions, auditability, and termination. Deliver one tested agreement and an exit exercise; do not assume vendor cooperation.
4. **Maintained-service pilot.** Offer an optional OER feed with metadata correction, provenance checks, documented refresh schedules, and support. Charge for maintained delivery and service commitments, not permission to reuse public-domain or CC-licensed works. Allocate eligible net service receipts through a published, community-approved rule. Test buyer demand through actual small contracts, not expressions of interest.
5. **Open protocols and independent assurance.** Publish mandate templates, receipt schemas, portable exports, service specifications, cost assumptions, evaluation methods, and negative findings. Release original documentation under CC BY 4.0 and new code under an appropriate open-source license after rights review. Third-party materials retain their licenses.

Public-domain status and existing CC freedoms remain intact. Preference signals may communicate expectations but do not create automatic payment obligations or retroactively rewrite licenses. [S08] [S09] The initial pilot excludes personal data, inboxes, learner records, and mixed private communications from service inputs and evaluation datasets. Necessary employment/payment administration remains separate, access-controlled, and minimized.

## 4. Roles and governance

A confirmed applicant or fiscal sponsor would hold funds, employ or contract delivery staff, and retain fiduciary responsibility. A community-majority steering group would approve scope, supplier selection criteria, stewardship allocations, and continuation recommendations. Its charter would include affected nonmembers, accessible complaints, conflict disclosures, recusals, and published decision summaries.

A delivery lead would coordinate procurement and operations; technical maintainers would run the service; qualified counsel would assess rights, competition, procurement, and organizational questions. An independent evaluator would report directly to the accountable governing body with publication protections. Evaluators would not evaluate their own delivery work.

Donors and vendors cannot buy votes, editorial control, preferential findings, or vetoes over adverse results. Governance authority must fit the sponsor's legal duties. No charitable form, tax treatment, or program-related investment (PRI) status is guaranteed.

## 5. Schedule and stage gates

Months run from an authorized grant start, not this draft's date.

| Period | Action and outputs | Gate before further commitment |
|---|---|---|
| Months 1–3 | Confirm host and locality; recruit coalition; establish baseline, charter, and safeguarding rules. | Lawful applicant, accountable signatory, community approval, and feasible delivery budget. |
| Months 4–6 | Co-design mandates; obtain legal review; solicit supplier offers and buyer demand; publish evaluation plan. | At least four willing institutions, comprehensible mandates, suitable offers, and no unresolved material rights issue. |
| Months 7–12 | Launch limited purchasing and maintained-service operations; issue stewardship microgrants; test payments and exports. | Independent review of quality, reconciliation, complaints, workload, and early demand. |
| Months 13–18 | Correct failures; test renewal and alternative suppliers; report full costs and community decisions. | Community supports continuation; credible benefit versus baseline; affordable forward obligations. |
| Months 19–24 | Complete evaluation and exit exercise; publish results; arrange stewardship or orderly closure. | Continue only with lawful governance, evidenced demand, and identified financing. |

Failure at a gate pauses new commitments while preserving essential obligations and an orderly exit. Disbursement tranches and gate authority require sponsor and funder agreement.

## 6. Evidence, outcomes, and stopping conditions

Before launch, community representatives and the evaluator would ratify measures and thresholds. Proposed tests include:

- **Agency:** at least 80% of participating representatives can explain mandate scope, refusal, and exit in a compensated comprehension exercise; failure triggers redesign, not participant exclusion.
- **Service value:** compare total purchasing and staff-time costs, quality, and contractual protections with each institution's baseline and contemporaneous standalone offers. Proposed continuation target: equivalent or better quality and either lower full cost or explicitly approved, costed improvements in protections.
- **Maintenance and demand:** record resource corrections, update timeliness, paid buyer contracts, and actual renewals. By month 18, seek two unrelated buyers willing to renew without pilot-funded purchase subsidies; distinguish willingness from received cash.
- **Value-return and accountability:** reconcile every transfer, publish aggregate allocation and complaint outcomes, and verify recipients performed funded stewardship work without requiring favorable findings.

A phased rollout could compare early participants with later entrants, where acceptable. Small samples, self-selection, and changing vendor prices limit causal claims; report uncertainty, subsidy dependence, and failures. Measure accessibility and distribution of benefits, not simply transaction volume.

Risks include vendor lock-in, unrecognized rights, capture by well-resourced members, low buyer demand, and administrative overhead exceeding benefit. Export tests, counsel review, paid representation, independent evaluation, and full-cost accounting mitigate but do not eliminate them. Pause immediately for prohibited data entering the service, material rights violations, unreconciled funds, or compromised evaluation independence. Stop or narrow the service if remediation fails, community authorization lapses, or renewal economics remain unsupported. No expansion to personal data is authorized by this grant design.

## 8. Sustainability without a revenue promise

Grant funds would pay for startup, inclusion, independent scrutiny, and public goods. Future earned income could cover a bounded share of maintained delivery, support, and procurement administration, subject to real contracts and full-cost review. Grants and earned revenue would remain distinct in budgets and reporting; receipts are not profit. Any grant-funded customer purchases would be disclosed and excluded from independent-demand claims.

The repository's separate **$660,000 operator-receipts planning model** is neither this pilot's forecast nor committed revenue. Operating deficits require explicit financing or scope reduction. Reserve retention is not an additional operating expense, and cash restricted or owed to others is not a freely available reserve. Do not assume recurring grants, commercial surplus, or an investment structure. If renewal cannot support operations, preserve open outputs and close the service responsibly.
