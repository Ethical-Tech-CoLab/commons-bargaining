# Commons Collective: a replicable institution, not a franchise

**Discussion paper · 18 September 2026 · version 0.1 · not peer-reviewed**

**Status:** The proposed **Commons Collective Federation (CCF)** exists only as a blueprint. It is not incorporated, operational, endorsed, or authorized to represent anyone. The **Commons Collective Framework Agreement** is the proposed framework supported by these original, nonbinding, annotated templates; they are not executed agreements or legally sufficient instruments.

## Abstract

Commons Collective proposes interoperable, autonomous sector collectives that purchase services, represent specifically authorized contributors, and finance shared knowledge without enclosing it. Replication means adapting accountable functions and shared protocols, not reproducing legal entities or creating a universal cartel. This paper distinguishes six sectors, specifies a fork/adapt/federate decision gate, and proposes a falsifiable, costed pilot. Its central hypothesis is that bounded collective mandates can improve service conditions and support commons maintenance while preserving refusal, public access, and independent governance. That hypothesis remains untested. Legal authority, community legitimacy, payment arrangements, and institutional form must be established locally before operation.

## 1. Method and research question

The question is not how many organizations to incorporate, but which missing functions justify collective action. This is a targeted primary-source comparison and normative design exercise, not a systematic review, legal opinion, or completed field study. Sector foundations were checked against the Budapest Open Access Initiative, Open Definition, UNESCO recommendations, Local Contexts, and journalist-safety guidance. Existing research supplies institutional and legal issue-spotting. The companion registers distinguish each source's supporting claim from its limits. [S70] [S71] [S72] [S73] [S20] [S74]

Ostrom's polycentric account motivates contextual, nested governance rather than one headquarters governing every commons. Data-trust proposals illuminate delegated responsibility but do not create universal ownership of data. Relational governance explains why people affected by a dataset need representation even when they did not contribute it. These are design resources, not proof that this federation will work. [S03] [S07] [S25]

The CoLab's field-grounded, prototype-first, open-by-default commitments inform a sequence: map existing institutions, pay affected people for design participation, test low-risk services, publish negative findings. No affiliation or endorsement follows from using these principles. Public methods must coexist with private member, patient, student, source, and community records. [S01] [S02]

## 2. Normative proposal: functions before entities

Every implementation needs six accountable functions, although several may sit in an existing organization with enforceable separation:

| Function | Minimum responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Scoped member procurement | Aggregate a specified service demand, document alternatives, and test delivered conditions. |
| Contributor agency | Verify authority, record purpose-limited mandates, and implement future-use withdrawal. |
| Independent commons stewardship | Allocate maintenance support according to public benefit, not buyer access privileges. |
| Professional operator | Run contracts, accessible support, records, security, and service delivery under replaceable delegation. |
| Independent assurance | Inspect authority, finances, rights, complaints, and outcomes without operator control over findings. |
| Regulated payments | Use appropriately regulated providers where required; reconcile, safeguard, and settle earmarked funds. |

Functional independence does not automatically require three corporations, a trust, or a charity. Specify decision rights, conflicts, accounting separation, inspection powers, and removal routes first. Counsel then determines legal forms, liabilities, tax treatment, capital rules, and whether separate entities are necessary. A commons fund has no assumed charitable status, tax exemption, or authority to hold client money.

The minimum viable collective can therefore be a hosted program purchasing preservation services, with independent stewardship and an outside payment provider. Existing library consortia, community archives, educational networks, or newsroom associations may already perform these functions better than a new organization. Replication should strengthen them, not manufacture competitors for scarce maintenance funding.

### Decision gate: fork, adapt, federate, or create

Before incorporation, publish a local institutional map and a gap assessment approved by prospective members and affected communities. **Fork** these openly licensed documents when a distinct context needs different rules. **Adapt** an existing institution when its mandate and accountability can legitimately cover the gap. **Federate** autonomous institutions when shared evidence or procurement interfaces solve a cross-institution problem. **Create new** only when documented alternatives cannot deliver necessary functions, a legitimate constituency requests it, and a sustainable operator and exit budget exist.

Record rejected options, duplication risks, consent evidence, and who can challenge the decision. Failure to establish authority, lawful funding, or an accountable home means stop, not incorporate by default.

## 3. A federation charter without compulsory membership

The proposed federation coordinates versioned schemas, rights vocabulary, assurance methods, accessible grievance routing, and voluntary shared service procurement. Each collective retains its own membership, treasury, operator selection, legal duties, and decision process. No collective can bind another without a transaction-specific delegation. Membership is nonexclusive; mandates are portable within their lawful scope; leaving the federation does not remove public access or existing open-license permissions.

The charter should prohibit cross-sector compulsory exclusivity, territorial allocation, federation-wide price floors, and an automatic boycott mechanism. Procurement and contributor negotiations require different legal analyses. Relevant EU guidance concerns specified solo-self-employed working conditions, not every licensing arrangement; US labor-law protections cannot simply be assumed for independent contributors. Counsel must assess relevant markets, information exchange, buyer power, and actual mandates before any price coordination. [S13] [S33]

Governance needs member voice, supplier voice, an affected-nonmember council, and independent stewards. Supplier voice is not access to rival bids or a veto over rights enforcement. Publish conflict declarations, capped terms, recall procedures, and a funded appeal channel outside the operator's management line. Require concurrent approval by the member body and independent rights stewards for protected-rule changes; no majority may waive another person's rights.

For natural-person members, use an equal-vote model subject to valid local law: neither purchasing volume, donor funding, nor contributed data buys additional votes. If a legal form requires membership capital, document lawful subsidies or solidarity funding without creating patron control or concealing member liability. If inclusion cannot be achieved lawfully, choose another structure rather than quietly excluding poorer members.

## 4. The rights and revenue boundary

Already open knowledge remains open. The collective must not enclose the public domain, retrospectively charge royalties for existing licensed permissions, or make public access conditional on paying a federation levy. Creative Commons licenses cannot be revoked merely because a contributor withdraws a mandate; license conditions and other rights still matter. Open Definition likewise distinguishes genuinely open permission from royalty-conditioned access. [S08] [S26] [S71]

Maintain three separate negotiation schedules: **paid services** such as curation, reliable hosting, support, and new commissioned work; **authorized new permissions**, only where identified parties control the relevant rights; and **voluntary commons covenants**, supporting maintenance without pretending to tax everyone's existing uses. A paid service must not secretly turn the underlying open work into a members-only asset.

Withdrawal ends authority to make new commitments from a stated effective timestamp. Existing contracts require an explicit expiry, termination, and continuing-obligations schedule. Future processing under consent must be addressed separately from copyright licenses, lawful exceptions, and other processing bases; prior lawful processing does not automatically become unlawful. Do not promise deletion from trained models. EU data rules do not create a saleable ownership title, and data-intermediation regulation does not supply missing consent or competition immunity. Research exceptions require jurisdiction-specific analysis. [S10] [S11] [S35]

## 5. Six sector adapters

Each adapter records constituency, legitimate authority, negotiable offerings, protected boundaries, benefit evidence, buyers, and a stoppable pilot. These are separate organizational candidates, not instructions to establish six new bodies.

### Open access: scholarly publication

Authors, editors, libraries, and scholarly societies can pool publication and preservation procurement. Authority comes from institutional purchasing delegations and verified author or publisher rights, not membership alone. Buyers include library consortia and funders; negotiable services include repository hosting, accessibility remediation, editorial infrastructure, and preservation. Open scholarly articles must remain accessible; publication funding cannot buy research conclusions or retroactive licensing power. BOAI identifies scholarly literature and its financing problem, not ownership over the entire research lifecycle. [S70]

Allocate benefits to equitable publishing access, editorial labor, and maintenance, reporting author charges and exclusion by institution and language. Pilot one repository-service contract; measure cost per preserved accessible article and deposit success. Stop for new access barriers, rights defects, or unremedied author exclusion.

### Open data: permission and nonrival reuse

Dataset maintainers, public-interest users, and affected people need distinct representation. Copying data is normally nonrival, while privacy harms and maintenance costs are real. Authority depends on dataset licenses, database rights where relevant, public-body duties, and valid processing bases; no one owns every fact about themselves or others. Buyers include municipalities, researchers, and service developers. Negotiate documentation, updates, secure computation, and properly authorized new releases, not ownership of public facts. [S71] [S10]

Allocate benefits to maintenance and affected-community priorities rather than rewarding sensitive disclosure. Pilot a documented nonpersonal dataset service; measure reuse, correction time, and reidentification risk. Stop when personal information appears, authorization fails, or a buyer seeks exclusivity over the open dataset.

### Open science: the research lifecycle

Researchers, research participants, laboratories, infrastructure maintainers, and communities span protocols, methods, code, instruments, evaluation, and outputs. This is broader than publication access. Authority combines institutional delegations, contributor rights, ethics review, and participant/community obligations. Universities, funders, and laboratories may purchase reproducibility support, compute, preservation, and technical assistance. UNESCO explicitly recognizes justified restrictions, including privacy and sensitive Indigenous knowledge; “open” is not permission to expose participants. [S72]

Support reproducibility labor and shared infrastructure, publishing benefit criteria. Pilot an already shareable workflow reproduction; measure independent replication success, maintenance time, and total cost. Stop for confidentiality breaches, invalid participant permissions, or pressure to suppress negative findings.

### Open education: learning without coerced participation

Teachers, learners, accessibility specialists, and institutions constitute the collective, with age-appropriate participation and safeguarding for minors. Authority over teaching materials differs from authority over student work, education records, or learner telemetry. Schools, universities, and public education purchasers can negotiate OER adaptation, translation, accessibility, hosting, and teacher support. UNESCO's OER framework supports reuse and equitable access, not extraction of student data. [S73]

Protect teacher academic freedom, student refusal, accessible offline alternatives, and children's rights. Allocate benefits to paid adaptation, accessible materials, and underserved learners, not engagement surveillance. Pilot an accessible OER module; measure completion, teacher workload, access gaps, and learning outcomes with minimal data. Stop if participation affects grades or essential access, or safeguarding review fails.

### Open cultural heritage: custody is not authority

Indigenous peoples, source communities, artists, archives, and museums require locally legitimate roles. Holding an object or scan does not establish copyright ownership, community authorization, or permission to disclose sacred or sensitive material. Local Contexts' community-grounded protocols help communicate responsibilities; labels are not generic commercial licenses. [S20]

Museums, public cultural agencies, and researchers may buy conservation, community-directed description, digitization, or approved access services. Community authorities determine applicable consent and exclusion processes; public-domain status is not retrospectively rewritten. Allocate benefits to community-defined stewardship and paid cultural expertise, with confidential evidence where necessary. Pilot only community-approved nonsensitive material; measure provenance corrections and community acceptance. Stop on contested authority, sacred-material exposure, or unresolved repatriation/custody disputes.

### Open journalism: public interest and editorial independence

Journalists, independent editors, newsrooms, and underserved publics have overlapping but different interests. Newsroom purchasing authority, freelancer rights, and source confidentiality cannot be collapsed into a publisher's blanket mandate. Buyers include newsroom networks, libraries, and public-interest funders. Negotiate secure tools, preservation, accessibility, translation, and specifically authorized syndication. Not all journalism, interview notes, or source material is automatically open; public interest is not a universal reuse license. CPJ emphasizes threat assessment and protection of sources and communications. [S74]

Keep funders and buyers outside editorial decisions. Allocate benefits to reporting capacity, source protection, and underserved coverage without paying for favorable stories. Pilot secure transcription using fictional interviews; measure accuracy, confidentiality, editorial autonomy, and net cost. Stop for source exposure, compelled editorial influence, or unauthorized training reuse.

## 6. Falsifiable pilot and evaluation

Start with one lawful service bundle in one sector and locality, not an economy-wide licensing pool. Preregister baseline alternatives, a quality-adjusted total-cost measure, hypotheses, and stopping rules before supplier selection. Include legal, assurance, administration, member time, accessibility, transition, payment fees, and funded exit costs. Report one-time grants separately from recurring economics.

**Proposed hypothesis:** by day 180, collective procurement reduces quality-adjusted total cost by at least 10% against a documented comparable alternative without worsening rights, access, or service quality. Separately, at least 90% of participating adults should correctly explain the mandate and withdrawal rule. These are proposed decision thresholds, not established effects. Report sample size, attrition, missing observations, and uncertainty; a small pilot establishes feasibility, not population-wide causality.

At the **90-day gate**, independent reviewers require complete authority records, all applicable approvals, a reconciled restricted-funds ledger, funded complaints, a withdrawal drill, and no unresolved serious incident. Otherwise pause contracting and remediate or exit. At the **180-day gate**, publish outcomes, distributional effects, complaints, service failures, reserve sufficiency, and the cost comparison. Missing baseline evidence means the savings hypothesis is untested, not achieved.

Immediately suspend affected activity for a serious confidentiality breach, invalid mandate, unresolved safeguarding concern, or unlawful coordination. The operator cannot overrule the independent stop authority. A failed economics threshold blocks expansion unless a transparent, separately justified public-benefit subsidy is approved; it must not be relabeled savings. Succession transfers only authorized records and obligations, with replacement-operator consent checks. Wind-down preserves required records, pays legitimate liabilities, returns restricted balances according to their terms, and maintains public access to already open outputs.

## 7. Limitations and reusable contribution

The proposal has no observed bargaining results, demonstrated legitimacy, or validated cost estimates. Primary institutional documents establish principles, not universal enforceability; jurisdiction, labor status, culture, and market concentration materially change the analysis. Representation itself can reproduce exclusion, and public financial reporting can expose vulnerable people unless carefully aggregated. No template resolves these problems automatically.

The contribution is a testable institutional grammar: separate functions, verify authority, preserve the commons, permit independent forks, and evaluate net benefits before scaling.
